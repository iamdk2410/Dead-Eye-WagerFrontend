SHOWDOWNS REIMAGINED: 'DEAD-EYE WAGER' SWEEPS THE NATION

Newfangled "Web-Camera" Contraption Allows for Duels of Skill Without Spilling a Drop of Blood.

A peculiar new craze is taking hold from the dusty plains of New Hanover to the bustling streets of Saint Denis. They call it 'Dead-Eye Wager,' a game of Rock, Paper, and Scissors fought not with pistols at dawn, but with hands in front of a screen.

Using a miraculous 'Vision-Engine' powered by modern ingenuity, the system captures a duelist's every move in real-time. "The machine don't lie," says one grizzled participant from Valentine. "You show your hand, it sees your play. It's the fairest game this side of the Lannahechee."

The rules are simple enough for any cowpoke to understand: challengers create a private 'saloon' online and send a coded telegram—or what they call a 'Room Code'—to their chosen rival. Onlookers and townsfolk can gather to spectate, watching the tense duel unfold on the digital scoreboard. It's all the thrill of a high-stakes showdown, with none of the grim consequences.
